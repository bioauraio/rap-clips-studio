# Editing: half the clip lives at the cut

Every previous lesson was about what happens inside the frame. This one is about
what happens between frames, and that is roughly half of how a clip feels.

Easy to test: take your finished clip and watch only the transitions. If every scene
simply gives way to the next, the clip reads as a slideshow even when every
individual frame is good.

## An editing device is a pair of scenes

An important difference from everything so far: an editing device **cannot be
applied to one scene**. It is always about two adjacent ones. So the pack from this
lesson lands in pairs: the first card sets the end of one scene, the second the
beginning of the next.

In the studio this looks like: the device is applied to scene N, and its last frame
becomes what scene N+1 has to arrive at. Check it on the contact sheet, not on
individual cards.

## The four cuts

### Cut on the movement

The character starts a movement in one location and finishes it in another. The arm
never stops moving, so the change of place reads as a device rather than a mistake.

This is the workhorse. Put it anywhere a jump between locations would otherwise look
accidental.

### Colour bridge

Two entirely unrelated scenes, but in both of them the same block of colour occupies
the same position in the frame. A red jacket in the centre, then a red sign in the
centre.

The device lets you cut between anything at all and look deliberate. **It is the
cheapest continuity that exists:** the location, the character and the time of day
can have nothing in common.

### Jump of scale

An establishing shot, then straight into a detail. Nothing in between.

This is the cure for a flat storyboard, but it only works as a pair. One establishing
shot on its own does nothing; an establishing shot followed by a fingernail does a
great deal.

### The still frame

One scene in the clip where absolutely nothing moves: not the camera, not the
person, not the air. Put it where the music drops out.

For the first two seconds the viewer thinks the video has frozen. Then the beat
returns and it turns out to have been on purpose. Silence is a device, and a clip of
uninterrupted movement has no dynamics at all.

Side benefit: it is the cheapest scene in the studio. Nothing moves, so the engine
has nothing to compute, and it works on any engine including the free one.

## Steps

1. Build the whole clip at least in frames and open the **contact sheet**.
2. Find three places where the transition between scenes looks accidental.
3. Put **cut on the movement** on the first, **colour bridge** on the second.
4. Find the quietest point of the track — a break, the pause before the last chorus.
   Put **the still frame** there.
5. Make sure the clip has at least one **jump of scale**: a wide shot straight into a
   detail.
6. **Regenerate frames** on the affected scenes — free.

## What to look at

**The sheet, not the frames.** Editing is invisible on a single card. Open the whole
storyboard and the repeats and dead transitions become obvious in ten seconds.

**The music.** The play button on a scene card gives you the slice of track under
that scene. A cut placed wrong is audible immediately — the device lands in the
middle of a bar instead of at the start of one.

## The usual mistakes

**All four devices in a row.** An editing device is a spice. Four in a row turn the
clip into a demonstration of devices rather than a clip. Spread them across the
running time.

**A colour bridge where the colours differ.** The device rests on exactly one thing:
the block must be **the same colour** and **in the same part of the frame**. An
orange block on the left and a red one on the right is not a bridge, it is two
unrelated scenes.

**A still frame on an expensive engine.** There is nothing to animate. Put the
cheapest engine your plan has on it — the result is identical and the credits stay.

**Cut on the movement between different characters.** The device works when the same
person in the same clothes continues the movement. Different people is not a match
cut, it is just a cut.

**Closing the loop without a first scene.** There is a separate device, "Closing the
loop" — the last frame repeats the first with one change. It is PRO, and it has a
hard requirement: the opening scene must be shot locked-off and remembered. If the
first scene is dynamic, you cannot repeat it last.

## The artefact

**Pack "Four cuts"** (`cut_basics`) — cut on the movement, colour bridge, jump of
scale, the still frame. Applied as pairs of adjacent scenes: a cut cannot be shown
on one. Slots: character, location, accent colour, detail. Free plan.
